# Research: what documented bots and traders say about making money on prediction markets

Compiled 2026-09-26. Sources are open-source repositories and write-ups; none of them publish audited P&L,
so treat every "edge" as a hypothesis to verify with our own trade log.

## Who is documenting what

| source | what it is | what we learned |
|---|---|---|
| [isaiahnick/ncaam-live-trader](https://github.com/isaiahnick/ncaam-live-trader) | Live NCAA basketball trader on Kalshi, Bayesian win-probability model, 2.8M in-game snapshots | The most rigorous live design found. Edge = model − ask, with a **required edge that ramps from 5% at tipoff to 15% late** because market makers see microstate the model cannot. Filters: spread ≤ 3c, price 10c–90c, ≥ 4 minutes left, and an **ESPN freshness guard** (no trade unless the score changed within 15 s). **Settlement is fee-free**, so exits must beat bid − fee > model + option value (≈5c mid-game contested, ≈2c when decided). Market noise around the model is ~6.7c in contested states, 2.8c in decided ones. Convergence tests show the market moves toward the model 1.5:1 even at 60–90 s, i.e. some persistent edge beyond latency. |

## What this means for our bot

1. **Do not chase ESPN after a play.** Our loop is 2 minutes; the market is 30 s ahead of ESPN at best. Any live "edge" that appears right after a score is the market being right and our model being stale. Fix: only act on a **stable snapshot** (score and win probability unchanged across two ESPN reads ~20 s apart), and never on the first read after a score change.
2. **Demand more edge late in close games.** Copy the ramp: 5c at kickoff rising toward 10c+ in the fourth quarter for contested games; keep the 3c bar only for decided games (model ≥ 90%). Skip contested games in the last 4 minutes entirely.
3. **Hold to settlement.** It is fee-free on both venues. Exits should only fire when the bid clears model + a noise allowance (~5c contested, ~2c decided), which is what we do; keep it.
4. **Fees decide everything at this size.** Two-thirds of one bot's losses were fees. Use the venue's real fee (0.06 × p(1−p) on Polymarket US) in screening, keep the exact preview before each order, and prefer entries below 50c where the fee per dollar is lower only when the edge is real.
5. **Pre-game: sharp books, not our own model.** Every documented pre-game bot that tested its own model found the sharp line better. Our pre-game source is ESPN's DraftKings line; a multi-book vig-free blend (The Odds API free tier, 500 requests/month) would tighten it. Pre-game edges on Polymarket US are 1–2c today, so this is a later upgrade.
6. **Instrument like mlb-kalshi-bot.** Log the model price at entry, the closing/settlement value, and rejection reasons per game, so the first weeks tell us which situations pay. We already log rejection counts; entry model price and CLV are added with this change.
7. **Speed is the moat we do not have.** Market making, cross-venue arbitrage and latency plays need sub-second loops and dedicated hosting. On a free cron, our realistic edges are: decided-game holds priced under fair value, late clean-ups before settlement, and any persistent mispricing that survives a 60–90 s horizon (which the NCAA data says exists at ~1.5:1 convergence).

## Honest expectation

Across every source, the documented steady-state for a well-run small bot is single-digit percent per month with real drawdowns. Anything faster comes from variance, not edge. Our job is to keep the expectation positive and let compounding and the trade log do the rest.
